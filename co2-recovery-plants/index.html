CO₂ Recovery and Utilisation

From Fermentation By-Product to Valuable Resource

Fermentation generates significant quantities of carbon dioxide during alcohol production. Rather than releasing this CO₂ directly into the atmosphere, distilleries and breweries can recover and process it for productive use.

MEGA CO₂ recovery plants capture fermentation CO₂, remove impurities, purify and liquefy the gas to produce liquid CO₂ for suitable beverage, food-processing and industrial applications. The recovered CO₂ can be utilised within the facility or supplied to external customers, depending on the plant's production capacity and intended application.

CO₂ sources: distillery and fuel ethanol fermentation; brewery fermentation.

The Recovery Process

How It Works

CO₂ recovery process at a glance

  1. Foam SeparationRaw fermentation gas passes through a foam separator, which removes entrained foam and liquid droplets before the gas enters the recovery system.
  2. Gas HoldingThe separated gas is collected in a gas holder or gas balloon. This provides temporary buffering and helps balance variations in gas flow from the fermentation process.
  3. Gas WashingThe gas passes through a washing system to remove water-soluble impurities, including entrained alcohol and other contaminants, before further processing.
  4. Gas CompressionThe washed CO₂ is compressed to the required operating pressure for downstream purification and liquefaction. Cooling and condensate separation may also be incorporated into this stage.
  5. Purification and DeodorisingThe compressed gas passes through purification equipment to remove residual impurities and odour-causing compounds. MEGA's configuration uses potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) treatment and activated carbon purification.
  6. Gas DryingA dehydration system removes residual moisture from the purified CO₂. This prepares the gas for low-temperature processing and helps prevent moisture-related operational problems.
  7. CO₂ LiquefactionThe dried CO₂ is cooled using an ammonia-based refrigeration system until it condenses into liquid form. Non-condensable gases, such as oxygen and nitrogen, are separated from the liquefied CO₂.
  8. Final Purification and StorageThe liquid CO₂ passes through a stripping stage for further removal of residual non-condensable impurities. The finished product is then transferred to suitable storage tanks for subsequent dispatch or utilisation.
