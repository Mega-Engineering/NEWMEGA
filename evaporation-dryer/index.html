Selecting the Right Evaporation Plant

Every Liquid Behaves Differently

No single evaporator suits every duty. We choose the evaporator type, the number of effects and the energy scheme from the liquid's own behaviour and the plant's operating conditions.

  1. 01

    Steam Economy

    With energy costs rising, the number of effects and vapour recompression decide how much steam is needed per tonne of water evaporated.

  2. 02

    Scaling and Fouling

    Liquids that scale or crystallise need forced circulation or scraped surfaces to keep heat transfer steady between cleanings.

  3. 03

    Thermal Sensitivity

    Heat-sensitive products need short residence times and low boiling temperatures, which falling film designs under vacuum provide.

  4. 04

    Condensate Quality

    Efficient vapour-liquid separators and mist eliminators keep TDS low in the condensate, so the water can be polished and reused.

  5. 05

    Operating Hours and Access

    Annual running hours, cleaning cycles and maintenance access shape the layout, the CIP system and the choice of equipment.

How Multiple Effect Evaporation Works

One Charge of Steam, Used Again and Again

In a multiple-effect evaporation plant, the vapour from each effect heats the next effect, which runs at a lower pressure. Only the first effect needs live steam, so steam consumption falls with every effect added.

FeedSpent wash · Effluent · Process liquid
Effect 1Heated by live steam
Effect 2Heated by Effect 1 vapour
Effect 3+Lower pressure, lower boiling point
ConcentrateTo dryer, CMS or incineration
Last vapour → Surface condenser Condensate → Polishing and reuse

Evaporator and Dryer Technologies

Five Technologies, Combined to Suit Your Process

Our evaporation systems are tailor-made, standalone or integrated, and often combine falling film, forced circulation and agitated thin film stages for the lowest operating and maintenance cost.

Evaporator

Falling Film Evaporator

How It Works
Liquid flows as a thin film down the inside of heated tubes, with vapour and liquid separated at the bottom.
Best For
Low-viscosity and heat-sensitive liquids, high evaporation rates.
Benefit
Short residence time and high heat transfer at small temperature differences.

Evaporator

Forced Circulation Evaporator

How It Works
A pump circulates the liquid at high velocity through the heater, and it flashes in a separator.
Best For
Scaling, crystallising and viscous liquids, final concentration stages.
Benefit
Long run times between cleanings on difficult liquids.

Dryer

Agitated Thin Film Dryer

How It Works
A rotor spreads the concentrate as a thin film on a heated wall, turning it into a thick paste or powder.
Best For
Very viscous concentrates and solids recovery in ZLD plants.
Benefit
Self-cleaning scraped surface and continuous discharge of solids.

Energy Saving

TVR: Thermal Vapour Recompression

How It Works
A steam jet thermocompressor recompresses part of the vapour and returns it as heating steam.
Best For
Plants with motive steam available and moderate capacities.
Benefit
Higher steam economy with no rotating machinery.

Energy Saving

MVR: Mechanical Vapour Recompression

How It Works
An electrically driven fan or compressor raises the vapour pressure so it can heat the same evaporator again.
Best For
Sites with limited steam and competitive electricity.
Benefit
Very low live-steam consumption and low operating cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Multiple Effect Evaporator?

A multiple effect evaporator (MEE) is a series of evaporator bodies in which the vapour boiled off in one effect is used as the heating medium for the next. Only the first effect uses live steam, so each additional effect cuts the steam needed per kilogram of water evaporated.

Which Evaporator Type Is Right for My Liquid?

It depends on the liquid. Falling film evaporators suit low-viscosity, heat-sensitive feeds; forced circulation suits scaling, crystallising or viscous liquids; an agitated thin film dryer handles the final, very thick concentrate. MEGA selects the type after reviewing scaling tendency, thermal sensitivity, operating hours and maintenance access.

What Is the Difference Between TVR and MVR?

Both re-use vapour to save energy. Thermal vapour recompression (TVR) uses a steam jet to recompress part of the vapour; mechanical vapour recompression (MVR) uses an electrically driven fan or compressor, so the evaporator runs mainly on power with very little live steam.

Can You Concentrate Distillery Spent Wash?

Yes. MEGA evaporation plants concentrate raw and bio-methanated spent wash (vinasse) up to 70% using low-pressure steam or alcohol vapours from the distillation plant, as part of a zero liquid discharge scheme or ahead of a vinasse spray dryer.

Can the Evaporator Be Integrated with the Distillation Plant?

Yes. Distillation and evaporation can be heat-integrated so that vapours from one section heat the other, which reduces the total steam demand of the distillery.

Is the Evaporation Plant Automatic?

Yes. MEGA evaporation plants are fully automatic under PLC control, with CIP provision for cleaning and operator-friendly screens.
