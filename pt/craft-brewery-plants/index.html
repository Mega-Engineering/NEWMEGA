Engineering for Brewing Operations

Every Detail Matters in Brewery Design

A brewery requires more than a collection of vessels and equipment. Its performance depends on how effectively the brewing process, equipment configuration and supporting utilities work together. From malt handling and wort production to fermentation, maturation and cleaning, each stage must be considered when designing a brewery around its production capacity, beer styles and operating requirements. Careful coordination of process equipment, production capacity, utilities and plant layout forms the foundation of an efficient and well-organised brewery operation.

  1. 01

    Brewing Capacity and Production Requirements

    Brewery design begins with the required production volume, batch size and brewing frequency. These factors influence the sizing and number of brewhouse vessels, fermentation tanks and maturation tanks, as well as the overall plant configuration. Allowance for future production expansion may also be considered during the initial design.

  2. 02

    Brewhouse Process and Equipment Design

    The brewhouse converts malt and other brewing ingredients into wort, the liquid that is fermented to produce beer. Mashing supports the conversion of grain starches into fermentable sugars, while lautering separates the liquid wort from spent grains. Wort boiling, hop addition and subsequent clarification are further stages that require appropriately designed process vessels and equipment.

  3. 03

    Fermentation and Maturation Requirements

    After wort is clarified and cooled to the required pitching temperature, yeast is added to initiate fermentation. During this stage, yeast converts fermentable sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide. Fermentation vessel capacity, temperature control and the time required for fermentation and maturation must be considered when determining the brewery's tank configuration and production schedule.

  4. 04

    Utilities and Temperature Control

    Brewing operations require appropriately sized utility systems to support heating, cooling and equipment operation. Hot water and steam may be required for brewing processes and cleaning, while cooling systems help control wort and fermentation temperatures. Utility requirements depend on the plant configuration, production schedule and brewing process.

  5. 05

    Hygiene, Cleaning and Plant Layout

    Brewery equipment and product-contact surfaces require appropriate cleaning and sanitation to maintain hygienic production conditions. Equipment arrangement should also consider process flow, operator access, maintenance requirements, pipe routing and the available installation space. Cleaning-in-place (CIP) systems may be incorporated depending on the brewery's equipment and operating requirements.

Brewery Equipment & Systems

Explore Our Brewery Systems and Equipment

Explore the key equipment used across the brewing process, from wort preparation and cooling to fermentation and beer conditioning. Each equipment category serves a specific function in the brewery and contributes to the overall production process.

Brewhouse Equipment

The hot side of the brewery, where malt becomes wort: mashing, lautering, wort boiling with hop additions and whirlpool clarification.

Wort Cooling and Transfer Systems

Cooling the hot wort to pitching temperature and transferring it to the fermentation vessels, typically through a heat exchanger and transfer piping.

Fermentation Equipment

Fermentation vessels where yeast turns wort sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide under controlled temperature.

Beer Conditioning Equipment

Vessels for maturation, conditioning and holding the beer before packaging or serving.

Cleaning and Supporting Systems

Supporting systems such as cleaning-in-place, hot water, cooling and utilities that keep the brewery hygienic and running.

Brewery Engineering & Process Design

Integrated Engineering for Brewery Operations

Every brewery has specific production, space and operational requirements. MEGA Engineering considers these factors when developing brewery equipment configurations and plant layouts, aligning equipment selection and system integration with the intended brewing capacity and project scope.

  1. 1

    Brewery Plant Configuration

    Developing equipment configurations based on required brewing capacity, batch size, production frequency and the intended brewery operation. The layout considers equipment positioning, process flow and available installation space.

  2. 2

    Process Equipment Engineering

    Engineering brewery vessels and associated equipment to suit their intended process functions, operating conditions and capacity requirements. Equipment selection and specifications are considered as part of the overall brewery configuration.

  3. 3

    Equipment Integration and Piping

    Coordinating equipment connections, product transfer routes and supporting piping arrangements to establish a functional brewery installation. System interfaces are considered to support the intended sequence of brewing operations.

  4. 4

    Equipment Manufacturing

    Manufacturing brewery equipment according to approved designs and project specifications, with consideration for material selection, fabrication requirements, equipment dimensions and intended operating conditions.

  5. 5

    Installation and Project Coordination

    Coordinating equipment placement, installation interfaces and project requirements to support the implementation of the brewery plant in accordance with the agreed project scope.
